📌 Tips for a good report

  • ☀️Take the photo in daylight and show the whole garbage spot.
  • 📍Stand at the spot when you report, so the GPS location is correct.
  • 🏷️Choose the right problem type, because burning and dead animals are handled first.
  • 🗺️Add a landmark in the details, for example "behind the temple" or "near the bus stop".
  • 1️⃣One report per spot. If someone already reported it, yours is added as support (+1).
  • 🔖Note down your tracking ID to check the progress later.
  • 🚫Never send fake reports. The device can get blocked.

    🟢 Wet / Biodegradable

    Rots naturally in days or weeks. Food leftovers, vegetable and fruit peels, tea leaves, egg shells, flowers, garden leaves. Can become compost or biogas.

    🔵 Dry / Non-biodegradable

    Does not rot for years. Plastic bottles and covers, paper, cardboard, metal cans, glass, old clothes, rubber. Keep clean and dry so it can be recycled.

    🔴 Domestic hazardous

    Harmful to people and soil. Batteries, bulbs and tube lights, medicines, paint and chemical cans, sanitary pads and diapers (wrapped), e-waste. Never mix it with other waste.

    Simple tips for a cleaner Gudlavalleru

    Small daily habits make the biggest difference.

    Do's and don'ts

    ✅ Do

    • Keep separate bins for wet, dry and hazardous waste
    • Hand over waste only to the collection vehicle or worker
    • Carry a cloth bag when shopping
    • Compost kitchen waste at home if you have space
    • Rinse bottles and covers before giving them for recycling
    • Wrap sanitary waste in paper and mark it
    • Report garbage spots using this app

    ❌ Don't

    • Don't throw garbage on roads, in canals or in empty plots
    • Don't burn garbage, leaves or plastic
    • Don't mix batteries, bulbs or medicines with other waste
    • Don't use banned single-use plastic items
    • Don't throw waste into drains
    • Don't keep waste uncovered at home
    • Don't make fake reports in this app

    Why it matters for your health

    Garbage left in the open is a breeding ground for disease.

    🦟

    Mosquitoes

    Rainwater collects in thrown cups, tyres and covers. Mosquitoes breed there and spread dengue, malaria and chikungunya.

    🪰

    Flies

    Flies sit on rotting waste and then on food, spreading diarrhoea, cholera and typhoid.

    🐀

    Rats & stray animals

    Garbage attracts rats, dogs and cattle. Rats can spread leptospirosis, and cattle that eat plastic get sick.

    😷

    Burning waste

    Burning plastic releases toxic smoke (including dioxins) that causes breathing problems, asthma and other long-term illness.

    💧

    Water pollution

    Liquid from rotting waste (leachate) seeps into the ground and pollutes wells, ponds and canals.

    🌊

    Blocked drains

    Plastic blocks drains, causing waterlogging and flooding in the rainy season.

    Types of solid waste

    Solid waste is any discarded solid material from homes, shops, institutions, markets and public places.

    🏠

    Household (municipal) waste

    Daily waste from homes: kitchen waste, packaging, paper, plastics.

    🏪

    Commercial & market waste

    From shops, hotels, vegetable markets. Large amounts of wet waste and packaging.

    🌾

    Agricultural waste

    Crop residue, straw, husk and animal dung. Good for composting and biogas, and should not be burnt.

    🧱

    Construction & demolition

    Bricks, concrete, sand, tiles. Can be reused for filling and making new building blocks.

    🏥

    Biomedical waste

    Syringes, bandages and other hospital waste. Must be handled separately by authorised agencies.

    💻

    E-waste

    Old phones, chargers, batteries, computers. Contains toxic metals. Give it to authorised e-waste collectors.

    The waste hierarchy: best to worst

    The best waste is the waste that is never created. Dumping in a landfill is the last option.

    1. REFUSESay no to single-use plastic covers, cups and straws
    2. REDUCEBuy only what you need, choose less packaging
    3. REUSECloth bags, steel bottles, refill containers
    4. RECYCLEGive clean dry waste to collectors and scrap dealers
    5. RECOVERCompost, biogas, energy from waste
    6. DISPOSEScientific landfill, only for what is left

    Home / pit composting

    Wet wasteLow cost

    Kitchen and garden waste is layered with dry leaves and soil in a pot, bin or pit. Bacteria break it down into compost in about 1–2 months.

    • Turn it every week for air
    • Keep it moist, not wet
    • Don't add plastic, oil or meat
    🪱

    Vermicomposting

    Wet wasteFaster

    Earthworms (like Eisenia fetida) eat organic waste and produce vermicompost, a very rich natural fertiliser. Suitable for homes, schools and farms.

    🔥

    Biogas (anaerobic digestion)

    Wet wasteEnergy

    Food waste and cow dung are sealed in a digester without air. Microbes produce methane gas for cooking, and the leftover slurry is used as manure.

    ♻️

    Recycling

    Dry waste

    Paper, plastic, glass and metal are collected, sorted at a Material Recovery Facility (MRF), cleaned and made into new products. This saves raw materials and energy.

    🛣️

    Plastic in road making

    Dry waste

    Shredded waste plastic is mixed with hot bitumen to lay roads. Thousands of kilometres of plastic roads have already been built in India.

    🏭

    Waste-to-energy / incineration

    Non-recyclable

    Burning waste at very high temperature in controlled plants, with pollution filters, to produce electricity. Open burning at home is not this, and it is harmful.

    🏔️

    Sanitary landfill

    Last option

    An engineered pit with a plastic and clay liner, leachate collection and daily soil cover. It's used only for waste that can't be recycled or recovered. An open dump is not a landfill.

    🚛

    Door-to-door collection

    Collection

    Segregated waste is collected daily from each house in separate compartments, so wet and dry waste stay apart all the way to processing.

    🧱

    C&D waste recycling

    Debris

    Construction debris is crushed and reused to make bricks, paver blocks and road base material instead of being dumped on roadsides.

    Rules & government programmes

    📜

    Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016

    Make every waste generator responsible for segregating waste into three streams (wet, dry and domestic hazardous) and handing it to authorised collectors. Littering and burning waste are not allowed.

    🚫

    Single-use plastic ban (2022)

    Under the Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules, identified single-use plastic items like plastic cutlery, straws, earbuds with plastic sticks and thin carry bags are banned in India.

    🇮🇳

    Swachh Bharat Mission

    A national mission for clean, garbage-free villages and towns, covering door-to-door collection, segregation, and solid and liquid waste management in villages.
